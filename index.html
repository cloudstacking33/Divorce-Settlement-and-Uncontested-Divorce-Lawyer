Why Your Divorce Settlement Matters

A Divorce Settlement is more than a form you sign on the way out of a marriage. It can decide who keeps the home, how savings and retirement accounts are divided, who pays which debts, whether spousal maintenance is paid, and how parents will share time and costs. Once a judgment is entered, many of these terms are difficult to change. Careful drafting at the start protects you long after the case is closed.

As a family law attorney practice serving Staten Island, our focus is on turning your agreement into language the court can accept and that both people can actually follow. Vague promises such as splitting things evenly later often create new arguments. Specific dates, amounts and responsibilities help prevent them.

Is an Uncontested Divorce Right for You?

An uncontested divorce is generally possible when both spouses agree to end the marriage and agree on every related issue, or when the other spouse is properly served and does not contest the case. The issues usually include property, debts, maintenance, child custody, parenting time and child support. If even one major point remains disputed, the matter may need negotiation, mediation or contested litigation before it can be finished.

Many New York couples file on no fault grounds. This requires a sworn statement that the relationship has broken down irretrievably for at least six months. Neither person has to prove that the other did something wrong. The court still expects the financial and parenting issues to be resolved before it grants a judgment, and New York residency rules must also be met.

Frequently Asked Questions

What makes a divorce uncontested in New York?

Both spouses agree to the divorce and all related issues, or the served spouse does not contest it. Property, debt, maintenance, custody and support questions must be properly addressed before judgment.

What are the grounds for a No Fault Divorce?

One spouse states under oath that the marriage has been irretrievably broken for at least six months. No proof of wrongdoing is required, but related financial and parenting issues must be resolved.

What are the New York residency requirements?

Depending on where you married, where you lived as a couple and where the grounds arose, a one year or two year residency period may apply. In some cases, both spouses being residents when the case starts is enough if the grounds arose in New York. A lawyer can check which rule fits you.

How long does an uncontested divorce take?

There is no guaranteed timeline. It depends on service, signatures, the accuracy of the papers and the court's workload. Corrections to a filing can add time.

How much are the court fees?

Basic court filing fees for a New York divorce are commonly listed at about $335, with possible extra costs such as service, notary or certified copies. Fees can change, so confirm them with the court. Legal fees are separate, and fee waivers may be available to people who qualify.

Can one lawyer represent both spouses?

A lawyer should represent only one spouse in a divorce because the two people have separate interests. The other spouse may hire their own lawyer or choose to proceed without one.

What if my spouse will not sign or cannot be found?

A case may still be able to move forward. A spouse who is properly served and does not respond may be treated as in default. If your spouse cannot be located, you may need court permission to use another method of service.

These answers give general information about New York law and are not legal advice for your specific case.

No Fault Divorce

A no fault divorce in New York allows one spouse to state under oath that the marriage has been irretrievably broken for at least six months. You do not need to prove adultery, cruelty or another type of misconduct under this ground. That can keep the focus on future arrangements.

No fault does not mean every issue is automatically agreed. Equitable distribution, maintenance, child support, custody, parenting time and counsel fees must be resolved by agreement or decided by the court before judgment. Spouses can use the no fault ground and still have a contested case about money or children.

Divorce Mediation

Divorce mediation uses a neutral person to help spouses discuss open issues and look for agreement. The mediator does not decide the case or act as either spouse's personal lawyer.
